During my co-op at SFU Graduate Studies as a Communications & Marketing Associate, I worked across digital and print — from restructuring 50+ web pages in AEM to designing orientation materials for 400+ incoming students. Every deliverable had a real audience: faculty, staff, and graduate students across SFU's three campuses.

Document · 01
Interactive Document Redesign

I updated and maintained administrative documents across Graduate Studies — converting non-fillable files to interactive PDFs in InDesign and revising content based on requests from other teams, all within SFU's brand guidelines.

Fig.1: Digitally fillable PDF forms created in InDesign
Web · 02
AEM Web Content Management

I updated and restructured over 50 web pages in AEM, particularly during the rebranding from "Graduate and Postdoctoral Studies" to "Graduate Studies." This included full layout revisions for the Indigenous Graduate Students page and the 3MT (Three Minute Thesis) event page, improving readability and information flow.

Fig.2: Indigenous Graduate Students page — long scroll split into sections
Fig.3: 3MT event page — key info made immediately visible
Previous layout hid essential content behind toggles. The new structure makes key information immediately visible, with expandable sections for optional details.

Visual Content: Banners & Email

I designed promotional banners for award pages and landing banners for key content areas. I also updated the welcome email sent to over 1,000 new students, including custom artwork for each SFU campus and acknowledgment of Indigenous territories.

System · 04
Student Profile System Optimization

To streamline the process of publishing graduate student profiles, I updated an internal HTML-based tool and documented the workflow for future team members. The updated tool enabled us to quickly generate over 100 student profile web pages. I also proposed and designed a standardized photo background to improve visual consistency across profiles.

Takeaway

This co-op showed me that design in an institutional setting is less about aesthetics and more about reducing friction — for the student filling out a form, the staff updating a web page, or the new arrival reading a welcome email. That shift in perspective — from 'does this look good?' to 'does this actually help someone?' — is something I carry into every project now.
